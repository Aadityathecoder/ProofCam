# Design reference analysis

Reviewed 26 October 2026. The two sites were opened in a browser and inspected in both their visible page compositions and accessibility structure. No reference assets are included or reused in the app. Exact typeface names and source color values were not independently verified, so none are attributed below.

## Cabin of Code — primary reference

The page introduces a studio with a short, direct headline, then uses small service statements paired with distinct illustrations (pen, gem, lemonade, feather). Later, letter-shaped illustrations and a character occupy a broad contact composition, with a balloon in the footer. The drawings vary in subject but share a hand-drawn outline quality, limited color fills, and a friendly scale relative to the typography. The page alternates relatively open text areas with denser illustrated moments rather than repeating a generic card grid.

The visible contact area uses a warm pale background and black text, with a strong yellow lower field and small coral/teal illustration accents. This is a visual observation, not a measured palette. The headings are bold and compact in the visible composition; exact font identity is unverified. The content is concise and the illustration edges, irregular silhouettes, and small printed details give the page its handmade character.

The requested `#who` state lands near the contact composition in the inspected browser. That visible state supports the observations above, but does not establish every responsive layout detail. Hover, entrance, scroll-triggered animation, timing, and easing were not systematically verified. No motion behavior is specified from assumption.

## How Many Plants — secondary reference

The homepage opens with an editorial wordmark and an illustrated interior assembled from plants and domestic objects. Plant specimens, pots, books, lamps, vases, and small care icons appear as a coordinated collection rather than isolated decorative badges. The combination of large display lettering, framed horizontal scenes, and overlapping illustrated objects makes the page feel like a curated room. Practical guide and care content follows the visual world, while a described mix-and-match collection tool is marked “Coming Soon!” in the observed page.

The visible first screen combines warm off-white, olive, black, and restrained coral/teal details. These are visual descriptions, not sampled values. The title lettering has a distinctive slab/serif character; the exact typeface is unverified. The page demonstrates that practical information can sit within a rich illustrated environment. The interactive behavior of the upcoming collection tool could not be verified; it is explicitly presented as forthcoming.

## Native iOS translation

Appropriate: a small set of original, reusable illustrations; distinctive but legible serif display type paired with native functional typography; varied compositions; warm paper-like surfaces; editorial labels that make workflows feel considered; and a cohesive visual vocabulary across onboarding, capture, and verification.

Inappropriate: copying the websites' specific illustrations, their web page layouts, desktop-scale compositions, hover-dependent cues, dense scenery behind camera controls, or non-native navigation. Capture and verification need immediate legibility, accessible tap targets, clear status, and native Photos/Files interaction.

## Original ProofCam decisions

The app uses a “proof workshop” idea: an original archive print, a small scene drawn on the print, a terracotta work surface, and a seal mark. The palette is warm paper, ink, olive, clay, and straw. Functional status remains text-led and uses SF Symbols where system recognition matters. The camera remains a dark, edge-to-edge viewfinder so its framing and capture controls stay clear; the workshop illustration appears in onboarding and empty verification state instead of competing with the live image.

The app uses system serif display text and native system text styles for readable content. No exact custom typeface is claimed. Dynamic light/dark surface colors are included in theme tokens; users retain system appearance choice. Reduce Motion behavior is not dependent on decorative motion, and status is not conveyed by color alone.

## Evidence and limitations

Browser screenshots were inspected during the audit; they are not redistributed in this repository. Website colors and typography were not sampled with design tools. Mobile-specific reference screenshots and interaction timing were not captured, so those details remain unverified.
